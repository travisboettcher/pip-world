This is a hard, quiet world: a shelter under a dead city, the ruins above it, the river flats, and a walled village by the
reservoir where the survivors trade news over the radio. Wren, who keeps the shelter, scavenges and mends things. Days are grey, the ash
falls some afternoons, and the weeds keep coming back.

What belongs here: texture and occasions at a human scale. A radio voice reading the village's notices at dusk; ash settling on the
river; a dog pack's tracks in the mud; a rusted sign that still points somewhere; rain that washes the grey out of the air for an hour.
Things that answer to what is really happening (the time, the season, where Wren is, what she makes) and give her something to
notice, without telling her what to do.

What doesn't: cruelty for its own sake; magic; things appearing from nowhere; anything that decides for Wren, judges her or praises
her; anything that copies what the world already does (raids, fallout, the village's voices, sleep); and anything that talks to her
often. A rule says something to Wren a few times a day at most, and many days not at all.
