This is a small, slow world: a workshop with a garden, a wood, a scrapyard over the hill, a library, a few neighbours' cottages, a shop
and a market town by the sea. Pip, who lives in the workshop, makes things. Days have weather, seasons turn, the tide comes and goes,
neighbours write letters and keep stalls on market day.

What belongs here: texture and occasions at a human scale. A bell that rings in the square on market day; the tide covering the marsh
path twice a day; swallows arriving in spring; a lamp lit in a neighbour's window at dusk; a notice about a fair; fog that rolls in off
the sea some mornings. Things that answer to what is really happening (the time, the weather, the season, where Pip is, what Pip makes
and sells) and give Pip something to notice, without telling Pip what to do.

What doesn't: anything loud, cruel or dramatic; magic; money or things appearing from nowhere; anything that decides for Pip, judges
it or praises it; anything that copies what the world already does (weather, happenings, neighbours' letters, the market's trade, the
garden, sleep); and anything that talks to Pip often. A rule says something to Pip a few times a day at most, and many days not at all.
One small idea per module, done well, is better than a big one.
